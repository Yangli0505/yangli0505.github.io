Yang Li

Associate Professor · College of Mechanical and Vehicle Engineering, Hunan University

I study how intelligent vehicles anticipate human behavior and make safe decisions on roads shared with pedestrians and other drivers.

I earned my PhD in Mechanical Engineering from Tsinghua University in 2020, including a research stay at California PATH, UC Berkeley. From 2020 to 2022, I was a postdoctoral researcher at TU Delft in the Netherlands, working with Prof. Matthijs Spaan. I am now an associate professor and master’s supervisor at Hunan University. In recent years, I have published 51 journal and conference papers, including 26 articles in SCI-indexed journals. I am first or corresponding author on 17 of those 26 articles.

My work has appeared in IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Transportation Systems, IEEE Transactions on Image Processing, Transportation Research Part C: Emerging Technologies, and IEEE Internet of Things Journal. One paper was designated an ESI Highly Cited Paper. My work has also received a best paper award at an international conference.

Research

Trajectory prediction and end-to-end planning

I model interactions among pedestrians and vehicles to predict several plausible trajectories in the same scene. In end-to-end planning, I study how road geometry, road-user type and path curvature shape the trajectory of the automated vehicle.

Safe decision-making and reinforcement learning for single and multiple agents

When should a vehicle yield at an on-ramp, and how should several vehicles coordinate a merge? I study how safety constraints and risk estimates can guide reinforcement learning to balance collision risk and traffic efficiency.

Risk modeling and ODD/ODC theory

I model driving risk to identify safety boundaries under different operational design conditions (ODCs). As a vehicle approaches or leaves its operational design domain (ODD), I study how to compare the risks of available maneuvers and choose the lowest-risk action.
